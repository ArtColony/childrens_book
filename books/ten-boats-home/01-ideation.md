# Stage 1: Ideation

## Brief

- **Audience:** ages 3–6, read aloud by an adult.
- **Format:** 8.5 in square, about 14 story pages plus a cover and an end page.
- **Medium:** the house watercolour-and-ink style in PostScript (`cb_kit`).
  It is strong at light, sky, water, repetition and simple stylised figures.
  It is weak at crowds, hands and detailed faces.
- **Avoid:** repeating *Moss Goes to the Sea*. Moss is a journey, with an
  animal hero, and its heart is "small and slow is fine". This book needs
  another shape and another heart.

## Scoring criteria (fixed before scoring)

Each criterion is scored 1–5.

1. **Payoff:** does the ending answer the opening want with feeling?
2. **Page turns:** does the shape make a reader *want* to turn?
3. **Visual variety:** can the pages differ in light, distance and composition?
4. **Feasibility:** can the house style draw every page well?
5. **Distinctness:** how different are the shape *and* the heart from Moss?

## Concepts

### A. *Tink's Light*: cumulative

> A firefly with the smallest light in the meadow lends it to a lost beetle,
> then a moth, then a mouse, and each one she helps begins to glow, until
> the whole meadow is lit.

- **Arc:** Tink wants a light that matters. She fears hers is too small,
  shares it anyway, and finds that shared light multiplies.
- **Shape:** cumulative, with one more light on each page.
- **Refrain:** "Follow me," said Tink.
- **Feasibility:** excellent, since glow on darkness is this medium's best
  trick. But the insects have to be drawn, and there are many of them.
- **Heart, roughly:** sharing your light makes more light, not less.
  Distinct in idea, but the "smallest one" set-up echoes Moss.

### B. *Ten Boats Home*: counting, then problem and solution

> Every evening Ana counts the fishing boats home, waiting for Grandpa's
> little blue one. Tonight nine come in, then fog, and Ana carries the old
> lamp to the end of the pier to light his way.

- **Arc:** Ana wants Grandpa home. The fog hides the harbour and he
  doesn't come. She chooses to *do* something with her waiting: she
  carries the lamp into the fog and holds it up. A light answers.
- **Shape:** a counting book (one, two, three … nine) that breaks at nine and
  turns into a problem with a solution. The count resumes at "Ten" when he
  comes home.
- **Refrain:** "Not Grandpa's." It changes at the end to "And this one is
  Grandpa's."
- **Feasibility:** good. Boats, water, sky, fog and a lamp glow are all in
  the medium's range. The risk is Ana, a human child, which is the hardest
  figure so far. She can be stylised: a red raincoat with a hood, seen in
  profile. **The hero sheet must prove her.**
- **Visual variety:** the sky moves from gold to pink to violet, then fog,
  then night with one warm light. Distance moves from wide harbour shots to
  close-ups of Ana, the lamp and the boat.

### C. *Bear Can't Find the Quiet*: problem and solution, there and back

> Bear wants perfect quiet to fall asleep. He leaves his noisy den for
> the hill, the lake and the cave, each quieter and lonelier, and comes back
> to sleep among the snoring, chirping family he left.

- **Arc:** the want (perfect quiet) is wrong. The real need is company.
- **Shape:** there and back again, with escalating places.
- **Refrain:** "Too loud," said Bear.
- **Feasibility:** poor to fair. A family of animals, a bear's body and
  sleeping poses are all outside the medium's comfort.
- **Heart, roughly:** home is noisy because it's full.

## Scores

| | Payoff | Page turns | Variety | Feasibility | Distinctness | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Tink's Light | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | **19** |
| B. Ten Boats Home | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | **23** |
| C. Bear Can't Find the Quiet | 4 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 4 | **18** |

**B wins.** Counting gives a reader an automatic reason to turn ("what
comes at five?"), and the break at nine turns that habit into suspense. It
has the best spread of light in the set. Its shape (counting, then problem
and solution) and its heart (below) are both far from Moss. Its one risk,
drawing a child, is exactly what the hero sheet is for. Concept A is kept
for later: it is a strong book, but its opening is too close to Moss's.

## The heart

- **For the adult:** *When someone you love is out of reach, you can turn
  waiting into doing: keep a light on for them.* Love isn't only feeling;
  it's an action taken in the dark.
- **For the child:** the feeling that **I can help, even though I'm small**,
  and that the dark and the fog are not stronger than a light someone holds
  up for you.
- **The question:** *What can you do while you wait for someone?*
- **What it is not:**
  - **Not "don't worry".** Ana's worry is real and the book respects it;
    the fog is genuinely frightening. The answer is action, not denial.
  - **Not "the child is responsible for a grown-up's safety".** Grandpa
    knows the sea and is coming home anyway. The lamp makes it *easier and
    sooner*, and he says so. It isn't what saves his life. The text must
    not suggest he would have been lost without her.
- **Where the hero acts it:** Ana takes the lamp from its hook and walks the
  pier into the fog (the choice), then holds it up (the image). That
  spread gets the book's strongest page.

## World note

- **Setting:** a small stone harbour with one long wooden pier. A lamp hook
  at the near end, an iron post at the far end. A village with lit windows
  behind.
- **Time:** one evening, in order: golden hour, sunset, dusk, fog, night.
  The light only moves forward.
- **Palette:** harbour blues and stone greys, with sails in muted, distinct
  colours for counting (red, green, ochre, white, rose, teal, violet, brown,
  orange) and **Grandpa's boat blue**. **One accent: lamp gold**, which
  appears small on page 1 (the lamp on its hook), then grows to fill the
  fog.
- **Cast:**
  - *Ana*, about five: red raincoat with a hood, yellow boots, seen mostly
    in profile.
  - *Grandpa*: a fisherman with a blue cap and a white beard, seen small in
    his boat, then on the pier.
  - The *boats*: one per count, each with its own sail colour.
- **Hero sheet:** `03-storyboard/hero_sheet.ps`, drawn by `lib/cb_ana.ps`.
  It shows Ana standing, walking, holding the lamp out and up, and seen
  close; Grandpa; a counting boat, Grandpa's boat and its lantern; the lamp
  glow in fog.
