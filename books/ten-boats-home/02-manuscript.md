## The text, read aloud

> Ten boats sail out in the morning. Every evening, Ana counts them home,
> while Mama mends the nets.
>
> Most of all, she waits for Grandpa's boat. It is little. It is blue. And
> it carries a lamp, just like the old lamp on the pier.
>
> Red sail. *One!* Not Grandpa's.
>
> Green sail. *Two!* Not Grandpa's.
>
> *Three! Four! Five!* Not one of them is Grandpa's.
>
> *Six. Seven. Eight.* … *Nine.* Not little. Not blue. Not Grandpa's.
>
> The gulls go quiet. Ana waits for ten.
>
> But ten is not a boat. Ten is fog.
>
> The fog swallows the boats. It swallows the pier. "Where is Grandpa?"
> Ana whispers.
>
> Ana looks up at the old lamp. She lifts it from its hook. "Mama! Light it,
> quick!"
>
> Mama lights it. *Click!* And out they go, into the fog.
>
> At the very end, Ana holds the lamp up high. Nothing. Only fog.
>
> So she holds it higher.
>
> Out in the fog, something blinks back. A little light. A little boat. A
> little *blue* boat.
>
> *Ten!* Little. Blue. *Grandpa's.*
>
> Grandpa lifts Ana up. "I saw your light," he says. "Ten boats home."
>
> "*Eleven*," says Ana.

The end page has no words: the lamp is back on its hook, still lit, and
all ten boats are home.

**Word count:** 192 (`wc -w`; draft 1 was 244). No page is over 25
words, and every sentence on the fog-to-lamp pages (pp. 8–13) is 11 words
or fewer.

## Page plan

**Light** moves only forward: morning (p1), then gold, sunset, dusk, fog and night.
**Shot:** W wide, M mid, C close. **Travel:** boats sail ← (homeward); Ana
faces → (out to sea); the lamp walk goes → and the walk home ←.

The four right-hand columns are the **continuity ledger**. Each cell must
agree with its neighbours, and a contradiction is a bug. Draft 1's errors
(Ana "at the end of the pier", Mama appearing from nowhere, the lamp in two
places at the end, never ten boats in one picture) all show up as
contradictions in these columns.

**Pages:** a cover, 17 story pages and a wordless end page make 19 files.
That is longer than draft 1 (16 files). It was decided deliberately:

- The count needs one page per beat, so a child can point and count.
- "Nothing" and "higher" must be two pages, so the turn between them
  costs Ana something.
- The refrain's reversal needs its own turn.

A 32-page printed book carries about 14 spreads, so 17 single pages is a
little over. Accepted for a square single-page format.

## Page turns

- **1 → 2:** ten go out, and we learn which one matters, and that it
  carries a lamp. The solution is seeded on page 2, not handed over on
  page 10.
- **3 → 6:** each turn delivers a number. The refrain answers it, then
  tightens: "Not little. Not blue. Not Grandpa's."
- **6 → 7:** the count stalls. "Ana waits for ten" is the held breath.
- **7 → 8:** ***But ten is not a boat. Ten is fog.*** The break lands on the
  number itself, and the missing tenth *is* the fog.
- **9 → 10:** she *looks up at the old lamp*. The idea is hers, earned on
  p2 (Grandpa's boat "carries a lamp, just like the old lamp on the pier")
  and shown, not told: two lamps, so a light can call a light.
- **12 → 13:** "Nothing. Only fog." becomes *So she holds it higher.* Her
  second choice is braver than her first, and it costs her the turn.
- **13 → 14:** the answer blinks back, light to light.
- **16 → 17:** Grandpa counts "ten boats home, and one little light". Ana
  counts past the title: ***"Eleven."*** The child who counted everyone
  counts herself, and the book ends on her voice.

## The heart, checked again

- **Acted:** Ana looks up at the lamp (p10), lifts it from its hook herself
  (p10), carries it into the fog (p11), holds it up (p12), and when nothing
  happens, holds it higher (p13). Every physical act of the rescue-that-isn't
  is hers except the match. No narrator states a moral.
- **The obstacle tests it.** Waiting worked for nine boats and fails at ten.
  Raising the lamp fails once, and she does it again anyway.
- **What it is not:**
  - *A rescue.* Grandpa's boat carries its own lamp (p2, p14), so he is
    sailing home, not lost. His line credits her light ("and one little
    light") without saying he needed saving.
  - *Unsafe.* Mama is there from page 1. She strikes the match, and the
    glass door clicks shut. Ana carries a closed lantern. The art carries the
    rest: both hands, a rail, Mama in frame.
- **The adult layer:** "Eleven" makes Ana part of what came home tonight.
  The light that brought the boats in counts as one of them.

## Glossary for the art

These are words the child reviewer didn't know. The pictures must show
them clearly the first time they appear:

- *pier* (p1, the long wooden walk out over the water)
- *harbour* (the place the boats are moored)
- *nets* (p1)

Draft 1's *mast* and *wick* are no longer in the text. *Fog* is the word
to watch: p8's "Ten is fog" is a metaphor a literal five-year-old will
question ("Ten is a number!"), so the picture must make it true, with the
fog standing exactly where the tenth boat should be.
