# Manuscript review: parent

*`reviewer-parent` on Sonnet. Given the text and the page plan.*

## 1. Verdict
I'd buy it. I'd only hide it if the lamp problems below ship uncorrected, because I'd be editing aloud every night. Grandpa's last line gives me something on the twentieth read.

## 2. Line edits
- **p1: "Ana stands at the end of the pier."** This contradicts p7 ("cannot even see the end of the pier") and p10 ("all the way to the end"). A 5-year-old will catch it. *Rewrite:* "Ana stands on the pier and counts them in, with Mama on the bench behind her." That also stops Mama appearing from nowhere on p9, when until then Ana looks like a small child alone on a pier every evening.
- **p10: "Mama lights the wick."** It reads as an open flame. *Rewrite:* "Mama lights the wick inside its glass. Ana carries the lantern in both hands, step by careful step, and Mama's hands stay close behind."
- **p11: "And there, Ana holds it high."** "And there" is vague, and the plan shows tiny Ana alone in the grey. *Rewrite:* "At the very end, Ana holds it high." The art must keep Mama in frame.
- **p14: "with the lamp to light the way."** This contradicts p15, where the lamp is back on its hook, and has a child carrying fire again. *Rewrite:* "and the lamp keeps shining behind them", or "Grandpa carries the lamp."
- **p14: Mama is silent.** She never greets Grandpa. Add "Mama takes his basket."
- **p8: "How will he find the harbour in all this grey?"** That's an adult construction, and it's the scariest line in the book. *Rewrite:* "How will he find us in all this grey?" It's shorter and more personal, and it sets up "your light made it feel very close".

## 3. Concerns (priority order)
1. **Fire and the pier at night.** The pictures have to carry the safety: a glass lantern, a rail on the pier, both of Ana's hands on the handle, and Mama's hands visibly close.
2. **Continuity of place and lamp:** where Ana stands, where the lamp hangs and who holds it at the end all conflict. Fix these before illustration.
3. **The counting will be audited.** The art must show exactly five and nine, as distinct sails.
4. **Whisper page to bedtime.** The worry resolves within four pages and the ending is calm, so bedtime survives. Keep the fog cosy, not menacing.
5. **Adult layer:** it's quiet. The refrain is fun to perform, but the grown-up payoff is only Grandpa's line. Don't thin it further.
6. **Roles:** Mama as the capable adult is good, but her silence after p10 makes her a prop.

## 4. What works
- The refrain and its escalation: "Not Grandpa's" ×3, then "not one of them is little, and not one of them is blue."
- "A little light. A little boat. A little *blue* boat." It's a perfect read-aloud build, and the turn to *Ten!* pays it off.
- "I knew the way, but your light made it feel very close." It makes the gift generous, not a rescue.
