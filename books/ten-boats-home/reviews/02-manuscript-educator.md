# Manuscript review: early-childhood educator

*`reviewer-educator` on Sonnet. Given the text and the page plan.*

## 1. Developmental fit (ages 3–6)
- Sentences are mostly short and rhythmic. The longest is p10, "Ana carries the lamp, step by careful step, all the way to the end of the pier, and Mama walks right behind." It's fine for 5–6s, but I'd breathe twice in it for 3s.
- Words that need picture support: *harbour, pier, masts, wick, sail, fog.*
- The concept load is light: one-to-one counting, then a surprise.

## 2. Counting accuracy, page by page
- p3: one red boat, "One!" Match.
- p4: green boat plus the red one moored behind makes 2, "Two!" Match.
- p5: five boats, "Three, four, five," and "Not Grandpa's" three times for three new boats. Match.
- p6: nine boats, "Six, seven, eight, nine." Match on paper.
- Total: 9 not-Grandpa's plus Grandpa's makes 10. The arithmetic is exact.

**Risks where a child can't count along:**
- **p6 is the weak page.** Four new boats packed into "a forest of masts" defeat one-to-one pointing. Draw nine distinct hulls with gaps, perhaps in two rows (4 and 5), each with its own sail colour.
- **p5:** three boats arrive at once. Stagger their sizes and positions.
- **p7:** the nine are "half swallowed" by fog. Keep all nine countable, or a child recounts and gets 7.
- **The tenth is never seen with the nine.** No picture lets a child count to ten. Make p13 or p14 show all ten moored together, with the blue one lit.
- **Cover:** "boats on a gold sea" has no number. Make it ten, or deliberately none.
- **Blue and little:** no boat on p3–p7 may have a blue hull or be as small as Grandpa's, or "not one of them is little, and not one of them is blue" fails.

## 3. Emotional literacy
- Worry is shown (p8, a whisper, a close shot) but not named. It's good for asking "How does Ana feel?"
- Coping is acted, not preached: she remembers, asks, carries. Healthy.
- Mama is a good safety fix, but she isn't introduced until p9, so on p1–p2 Ana looks alone at the pier's end. Show Mama, or the lit house, in the background on p1.
- Mama lighting the wick handles imitation of fire-handling.

## 4. Group read-aloud
- **Join-in:** "One!", "Two!", the "Not Grandpa's" chant, and the big "Ten!"
- **Pause and ask:** p7 "What do you think will happen?", p8 "Where is Grandpa?", p9 "What could Ana do?"
- **Where I'd lose them:** p5–p6 each pack several numbers into one turn. I'd slow down and have children point.

## 5. Extension
Ten paper boats with different sail colours, and one little blue one: line them up, count them in, find Grandpa's. Or darken the room and let a child "light" a torch to bring the blue boat in.

**Recommendation:** Yes, for ages 4–6 (3s as a lap-read counting to five), once p5, p6 and p13/14 are drawn so every boat can be pointed at and counted to ten.
