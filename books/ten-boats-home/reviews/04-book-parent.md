# Finished-book review: parent

*`reviewer-parent` on Sonnet. Given only the 19 rasterized pages, as the book comes off a shelf.*

**Verdict:** I'd buy it. The "Not Grandpa's" refrain and the turn on "Ten is fog" are real craft. I'd hide it only if the counting and the lamp handling stay muddy.

**Line edits**
- **p2: "She waits for one most of all."** The next page is "One!", a different boat. → "She waits for one special boat."
- **p8: "swallows"** is the one word that will wake my 4-year-old. → "hides".
- **p9: "Light it, quick!"** is a bit panicky at bedtime. → "please".
- **p15: "Ten boats home. And this one is Grandpa's."** reads as ten plus Grandpa's. → "The tenth is Grandpa's."
- **p17: "Eleven."** I think this counts the lamp, but nothing says so. My 4-year-old will say "there were TEN".
- **p1:** the orphan "nets." alone on a line.
- **Last page:** add "Ten boats. One lamp. Goodnight."

**Concerns**
1. **Flame and water.** On pp. 12–13, Mama's hand is outstretched but never touches Ana. Put her hand on Ana's coat.
2. **The count** ends in a ten-or-eleven tangle.
3. **No reassurance at the scary beat** (p9).
4. **Art slips:** arms like stretched red ribbons; boats vanishing on p7; the cover shows Grandpa's boat, which spoils the reveal.
5. **Assumptions:** everyone has the same peach skin tone, and Mama does all the practical care with no voice.

**What works:** the refrain a 4-year-old can chant; the "Eight. … Nine." pause; "Ten is fog" as a small riddle; the palette dimming and returning to a calm, wordless night; "one little light".
