# Manuscript review: child (Maya, 5½)

*`reviewer-child` on Haiku. Given only the read-aloud text.*

**1. Favourite page?** "The one where it says TEN! Because I counted all the boats with Ana, one, two, three... and it was Grandpa's! Yay!" *[Counting with a payoff. The rhythm of the number pages works well for this listener.]*

**2. Scary, sad or worrying?** "The fog. It came in and Ana couldn't see the pier and she said, 'Where is Grandpa?' My tummy went wobbly. But then the lamp! It got better." *[The fog and "Where is Grandpa?" carry real suspense; the lamp resolves it on the next page, so the worry is brief and pays off.]*

**3. Words you didn't understand?** "What's a mast? And what's a wick? Is it like a wick on a candle? Is a harbour a big pond?" *[Masts, wick and harbour are unfamiliar. "Masts" is inside a sentence that is already long, so it's easy to miss. "Harbour" recurs and may be learned from context. A gloss in the art or the text would help.]*

**4. Boring or too long?** "The fog bit went on and on, with all the soft and grey and until and cannot. I wriggled. And 'Mama lights the wick, Ana carries the lamp, step by careful step, all the way to the end' was a very long one. My legs went funny." *[The long, comma-chained sentences on the fog and lamp pages are where attention drops. Split them into two or three short sentences. The counting pages are short and work.]*

**5. Pictures?** "I didn't see any pictures." *[Revisit at the storyboard: the "not Grandpa's" boats must look clearly different from the blue one.]*

**6. Page turns:** red sail: "Is it Grandpa's? No. Next!" · green: "Not him either. Keep counting!" · three to five: "Not, not, not... come ON, Grandpa!" · six to nine: "Lots of boats! Is it purple outside? Turn it!" · fog: "Oh no. Turn it quick, I want to see." · lamp: "Yes! Light it! Light it!" · ten: "TEN! That's Grandpa's! I'm clapping!" · last: "Hooray, they all went home. Can we read it again?" *[Strong page-turn pull. Sail colours should be visible in the art; children will look for them.]*

**7. Again tomorrow?** "YES. Because of counting to ten, and because the little light came back to the boat and Grandpa found the way. I want to hold the lamp too." *[High re-read appeal: counting, a reveal, and an active role for the child with the lamp.]*

**Overall:** Counting and the blue-boat reveal are the strengths. The fog-to-lamp section is the most exciting and the hardest to follow aloud, because of its long sentences and unfamiliar words (mast, wick).
