# Finished-book review, round 2: picture-book editor (fresh reader)

*`reviewer-writer` on Opus, a fresh agent, after the stage 4 revision. Page numbers are file numbers (p-NN = printed page NN−1).*

**Concept.** Familiar (*Hello Lighthouse*, *Owl Moon*, *Ten, Nine, Eight*). The fresh part is the counting book that breaks at nine. "But ten is not a boat. Ten is fog." is the best line in the book, and a real turn.

**Structure.** The fixed wide camera on pp. 4–9 does its job: the sky moves from gold to rose to violet to grey, and fog eats the same view. It is quietly strong. The count is still thin in the middle (3–5 and 6–9 sprint), and pp. 10–12 are procedure. **The big mistake is p-15 → p-16:** "A little light. A little boat. A little blue boat," with the boat clearly drawn, gives the reveal away a turn early.

**Language.** The refrain builds, then flips to "Little. Blue. Grandpa's.", which is real craft. Several words describe what the picture shows ("lifts it from its hook", "holds the lamp up high", "He lifts Ana up, lamp and all").

**The heart.** It is acted. "Nothing. Only fog." then "So she holds it higher." is exactly right, and p-14 is the strongest image in the book. Weakened by Mama's hand on Ana in nearly every frame, and by a face that still doesn't show fear.

**Text and picture.** The figures are stiff, like paper dolls. Grandpa's arm doesn't really hold Ana. The light is where the art sings (pp. 12–15, 19).

**The ending.** "Eleven," says Ana is a lovely, childlike surprise, **but Grandpa has just said it for her ("and one little light"), so the joke arrives explained.** On p-18 the picture doesn't make the eleven clear.

## Three changes
1. **Hide the boat on the blink page**, so the reveal happens on the turn.
2. **Let Ana make the ending:** "'I saw your light,' says Grandpa. 'Ten boats home.'" / "'Eleven,' says Ana." with Ana's lamp the brightest thing.
3. **Cut the descriptive words and close the middle sag;** redraw the fear.

**Score: 7/10.** "Make changes 1 and 2 and it is near an 8. A 10 would also need characters whose bodies and faces act fear and joy as surely as the light does."
