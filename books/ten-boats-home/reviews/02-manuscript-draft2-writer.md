# Manuscript draft 2 review: picture-book editor (fresh reader)

*`reviewer-writer` on Opus, a new agent that had not seen draft 1 or any review. Given the ideation and draft 2.*

**1. Concept.** It is warm but familiar. A child waits on the shore for a fisherman, there is fog, and a light is held up (*Hello Lighthouse* is the nearest shelf neighbour). The counting structure is what makes it new. The book's real invention is that a count breaks at nine and the missing number becomes the weather. That idea is good enough to publish.

**2. Structure and page turns.** The best turn is 7→8: "But ten is not a boat. Ten is fog." That line would get the book acquired. There are three structural faults:
- **The text never establishes ten.** Nothing says ten boats went out, so "Ana waits for ten" comes from nowhere.
- **The count collapses where it should build.** p5 crams in "*Three. Four. Five.*" and p6 races through "*Six, seven, eight … nine.*"
- **The lamp idea is unearned (p10).** "Then she looks up at the old lamp" is a solution the author hands her. Nothing has suggested that a light can call a boat.

**3. Language.**
- "Not Grandpa's. Not Grandpa's. Not Grandpa's." reads as mechanical repetition, not a building refrain. "Not little. Not blue." is the first variation that works.
- After p6 the refrain vanishes for nine pages.
- "Grey" appears three times in five pages.
- The book opens with Mama as the subject; it should open with Ana and her want.
- Apart from "The gulls go quiet", I hear little sound-play.

**4. The heart.** It is mostly acted, with "So she holds it higher" right and spare. Two things weaken it:
- "How will he find us in all this grey?" states the problem so neatly that it doubles as the author's set-up. Cut it.
- Mama lights the lamp, shuts the door, walks beside Ana and catches the rope. For a book whose heart is *I can help, even though I'm small*, the adult does every physical act except holding the lamp.

**5. Text and picture.** "In both hands", "can't see the end of the pier" and "out they go along the pier" describe the art.

**6. The ending.** "A little light. A little boat. A little *blue* boat" is lovely. It repays p2 exactly. "I always know the way home. But tonight, I could see it." reads like a disclaimer written to satisfy the brief. "And this one is mine", one page after "And this one is Grandpa's", is a second reversal that waters down the first.

## Three changes

**A. Establish ten, put Ana first, and seed the lamp through Grandpa's own lantern.**
> Ten boats sail out in the morning. Every evening, Ana counts them home. / She waits for one most of all. It is little, and it is blue, and it carries a lamp just like the old one on the pier. It is Grandpa's.

**B. Give the count its weight; let the child speak the refrain.**
> *Three!* *Four!* *Five!* … / *Six. Seven. Eight.* … *Nine.* Not little. Not blue. Not Grandpa's.

**C. Cut the disclaimer; let the count deliver the surprise.**
> He lifts Ana up, lamp and all. / "Ten boats home," says Grandpa. "And one little light." / "Eleven," says Ana.

The counting book counts past its own title, and the child who counted everyone ends by counting herself.

**Score: 6.5 / 10.** Fix A–C and this becomes an 8. To reach 10, the prose would need music on every page.
