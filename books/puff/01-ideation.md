# Stage 1: Ideation

## Brief

- **Audience:** ages 3–6, read aloud by an adult.
- **Format:** 8.5 in square, about 13 story pages plus a cover and an end page.
- **The message is explicit.** This book follows the process's
  [explicit-message variant](../../docs/process.md#explicit-message-books).
  The lesson is said in plain words, carried by a refrain, and stated
  outright on the last story page. *Ten Boats Home* hid its heart in action,
  and its first human reader didn't understand it. This book must not need
  explaining.
- **The art is Moss's.** It should look like *Moss Goes to the Sea*, which
  our reader liked better:
  - a simple non-human hero with no arms or legs to act, so feeling comes
    from eyes, mouth and shape;
  - a small hero in a big landscape;
  - daylight, clean shapes, and the world carrying each page;
  - no long run of grey or night pages, and no glow rings.

## Scoring criteria (fixed before scoring)

Each criterion is scored 1–5.

1. **Message clarity:** can a five-year-old say the lesson back in one
   sentence after one reading?
2. **Earned:** does the story *show* the lesson working before it is said,
   so the stated line feels true rather than preached?
3. **Payoff:** does the ending answer the opening want with feeling?
4. **Drawability in Moss's style:** can every page be mostly landscape,
   sky and weather, with a limbless hero?
5. **Visual variety:** can the pages differ in light, weather and distance?

## Concepts

### A. *Puff*: a small cloud

> Puff is the smallest cloud in the sky. "You're too small to rain," rumble
> the big clouds. When they drift away and a seedling in a dry field begins
> to droop, Puff squeezes out every drop she has, and the seedling lives.

- **Message:** *No one is too small to help.*
- **Refrain:** "Too small," rumble the big clouds. It turns at the end when
  the seedling, now a tall sunflower, says it back: "Not too small. Just
  right."
- **Shape:** want, then a refusal, then a need only she is there for, then
  the cost (she shrinks to a wisp), then a reward that is also science. The
  sun warms the wet field, mist rises, and Puff is whole again: the water
  she gave comes back.
- **Drawability:** excellent. A cloud with eyes and a mouth is simpler than
  Moss, and the pages are all sky, hills and weather, which is the medium's
  strength. Puff's size, shape and colour show her feelings: she puffs up
  proud, sags, and thins.
- **Risk:** "the smallest one helps" is a familiar premise (Aesop's Lion and
  the Mouse). The water cycle return gives it a turn of its own.

### B. *Moss and the Big Leaf*: cumulative

> Rain comes, and Moss shelters under her big leaf. A ladybird asks to come
> in, then a beetle, a bee and a mouse. "There's always room for one more,"
> says Moss. When a big wet toad comes, there isn't. So Moss gives the toad
> her place and goes out into the rain, happy, because snails like rain.

- **Message:** *There's always room for one more, if you make room.*
- **Refrain:** "There's always room for one more," said Moss.
- **Shape:** cumulative, with one more guest per page, then a twist.
- **Drawability:** the hero is proven, but the guests are not. A mouse
  and a toad need legs and bodies, which this medium handles badly, and the
  crowd under the leaf is the hardest picture.
- **Risk:** it echoes *The Mitten* closely, and the twist depends on
  remembering the first book.

### C. *Little Acorn*: seasons

> An acorn falls in autumn and wants to be a tree *now*. Winter, spring and
> summer each tell it to wait, and it grows, a little at a time.

- **Message:** *Good things take time.*
- **Drawability:** good seasons, but the hero spends half the book
  underground or as a tiny shoot, and that makes dark, empty pages.
- **Risk:** the message is close to Moss's ("slow is fine"), and nothing
  happens. Waiting is not a plot.

## Scores

| | Clarity | Earned | Payoff | Drawability | Variety | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Puff | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | **24** |
| B. Moss and the Big Leaf | 5 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 3 | **18** |
| C. Little Acorn | 4 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | **16** |

**A wins.** It has the clearest lesson, and every page is sky, which is
what the medium paints best. The cost Puff pays (shrinking) makes the
lesson more than a slogan. B is a good book that falls on drawing a crowd of
animals. It is kept for a later Moss book, with a simpler guest list.

## The message

- **The line, said in the book:** *No one is too small to help.*
- **Who says it:** the sunflower, on the last story page, back to the big
  clouds' "too small".
- **Where it is shown before it is said:** Puff's three drops lift one leaf
  (a small help, and it works), then all her rain makes the seedling stand
  up. The child sees it work before anyone says it.
- **The reward isn't praise:** Puff comes back. The water she gave rises
  as mist and makes her whole again. Helping didn't use her up.
- **What it is not:** not "give until there's nothing left of you". Puff
  thins but she is never gone, and the book shows her coming back the next
  morning. Nor is it "the big clouds are bad". They aren't cruel, only
  busy and grand, and on the last page they make room for her.

## The test (this book's stop rule)

Fresh reader agents get only the text: a child, then an adult. Each says
in one sentence what the book teaches. Both answers must match **"no one is
too small to help"** in meaning, both at stage 2 and on the finished book.
The editor's score is advice, not the gate.

## World note

- **Setting:** rolling summer hills under a big sky, with one dry field
  and a fence, and a farmhouse far off. The sky is the main stage.
- **Time:** one hot day, then a night, then the next morning. A coda
  happens some weeks later, when the sunflower is tall.
- **Palette:** Moss's daylight blues and greens. The dry field is straw
  ochre and turns green once it's watered. The big clouds are cream and
  slate. Puff is pure white with a pink cheek.
- **Puff:** a round cumulus with three bumps on top, two ink dot eyes, a
  small mouth, and pink cheeks. She is about 40–60 pt across, and the big
  clouds are 4–6 times wider. Her moods:
  - **hopeful:** eyes up, round and full;
  - **sad:** eyes down, flattened;
  - **effort:** eyes shut, cheeks puffed;
  - **thin:** a pale wisp with tired eyes;
  - **joy:** a big smile, bumps lifted.

## After the editor's review

The editor ([review](reviews/01-ideation-writer.md)) scored the concept
6/10. All four points were accepted:

1. **The nearest comparable is *Cloudette*** (Lichtenheld, 2011), where a
   small cloud revives a pond. Puff's own turn is the **cost and the
   return**. Cloudette is never used up; Puff is used up and comes back.
2. **The moral moves to the big clouds.** The ones who said "too small" say
   *"No one is too small to help."* The sunflower gets the chant: *"Not too
   small. Just right."*
3. **"Too small" is said three times before it turns:** by the big clouds,
   in Puff's head, and once more as she decides.
4. **The cost is softened.** She rains *until the seedling stands*, not
   until she is empty. At her thinnest she smiles, the dusk is warm, and the
   mist fills her on the very next page. The water's return is shown, with
   at most one line and no science words.

The scores above were the author's. The 5/5 for "earned" was inflated: it
can only be earned on the page.
