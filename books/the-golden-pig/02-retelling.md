# Stage 2: The retelling (draft 2)

*The Legend of the Golden Pig* / *금돼지 전설*. A bilingual retelling of
*Choe Goun jeon* (최고운전) for ages 6–9. The plan is in
[01-interpretation.md](01-interpretation.md). Draft 1's reviews are in
`reviews/02-retelling-*.md`, and what changed is logged in
[checkpoints.md](checkpoints.md).

**Budget:** about 30 English words a page, 38 at most. The Korean has up to
4 lines in the top band (20 pt, leading 1.42), and the English 4 lines in the
bottom band (19 pt).
`scripts` can't check the fit yet; the storyboard sets every page in real
type and checks it there.

**Korean register:** storytelling 해요체 (-었어요, -지요, -답니다), as in
modern Korean picture books. Dialogue is in the speaker's own register.

**Refrain:** *"The golden pig's child, they say."* / *"금돼지 자식이래."*
It comes on pages 7, 11, 16 and 24, and is turned on page 29.

## Page turns

- **4 → 5:** what is inside the rock? (the gold cave).
- **5 → 6:** her pouch hangs on a deerskin cord. What will she do?
- **7 → 8:** what will the father do?
- **14 → 15 → 18:** what is in the box? It stays unanswered for four pages, and page 15 ends the first sitting for a class.
- **19 → 20:** he listens; then the answer, on the widest page.
- **28 → 29:** the father's line, then the ending.

이 이야기에 대하여

《금돼지 전설》은 적어도 사백 년 된 옛 소설 《최고운전》과, 지금도 입에서 입으로 전해지는 이야기에서 왔어요. 최치원은 실제 인물이에요. 857년 신라에서 태어나 열두 살에 당나라로 건너가 과거에 급제했고, 이름난 문장가가 되어 돌아왔어요. 호는 ‘고운(孤雲)’, 외로운 구름이에요.

그는 정말 금돼지의 자식이었을까요? 옛이야기마다 말이 다르고, 이 책도 답하지 않아요. 우리가 아는 것은 그가 한 일이에요. 아버지의 마지막 말은 우리가 덧붙였어요. 해바라기는 신라보다 훨씬 뒤에 아메리카에서 왔지만, 희망의 꽃으로 심었어요.

```en
About this story

The Legend of the Golden Pig comes from Choe Goun jeon, a Korean folk novel at least four hundred years old, and from tales still told aloud. Choe Chiwon was real. Born in Silla in 857, he sailed to Tang China at twelve, passed its examination, and came home a famous writer. His pen name was Goun, "Lone Cloud."

Was he the golden pig's child? The old tellings disagree, and this book doesn't say. What we know is what he did. The father's last words are ours. Sunflowers came from the Americas long after Silla; we planted them for hope.

Say it: Choe Chiwon (chweh chee-won), Silla (shil-la), Woryeongdae (wol-yung-day), Gaya (ga-ya).
```
