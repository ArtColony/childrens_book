# Retelling draft 1: The Golden Pig (child)

*reviewer-child (Haiku), briefed as a 7-year-old, given only the English read-aloud text, 2026-11-14. Saved unchanged.*

Here is how I, a seven-year-old, would answer after hearing The Legend of the Golden Pig read aloud. Grown-up translations are in brackets.

1. **What is this story trying to teach?** "Don't just believe what people whisper about someone. Watch what they do." [The father says it on page 27: "I should have watched what you did." The story is about judging people by rumour versus by their actions.]

2. **Best page and boring or confusing parts.** "The best was page 20, the chick! The Emperor opens the box and there's a little yellow chick that goes *cheep*. That was funny." [The clever egg-for-a-box trick is a comic payoff.] "Page 15 was boring. Nobody could see inside a stone, so they just sat there. And page 19 was confusing: 'things change in the dark'? What dark? What did that mean?" [Pages 15 and 19 explain the riddle in abstract terms; a five-year-old needs the chick image to land before the explanation.] "Words I didn't know: magistrate, pavilion, charms, barren, minister, peonies, immortal, shudder, and Silla and Tang, which are places, I think." [Several of these are archaic or foreign terms with no picture to help.] "Page 23b is a funny name. Why is it b?" [A page number that breaks the sequence.]

3. **Scary or sad?** "The pig was scary. The owl and the wind were scary too. The baby on the sand was the worst, because his daddy just walked away. But it got better, because the birds spread their wings over him and the maidens brought him food. The pig died, so that was OK." [Yes: child abandonment, a predatory creature, and a death. Each is followed by rescue or justice, so the scares resolve.] "The end was sad, because they walked up the mountain and disappeared. But the hat was there, so I think it's OK." [The ending is ambiguous; "immortal" is probably too big a word for a seven-year-old to be sure about.]

4. **What was in the box?** "I thought it was a jewel, or a gold coin, or a snake that would bite. It was a chick!" [The Emperor's box is a riddle. The answer is "an egg, now a chick," which is a clever twist.]

5. **Would I ask for it again?** "Yes, but only the pig and the chick parts. I'd skip the stone box. Also, can we have a book where nobody is whispered about?" [The story is good and has strong images, but the middle is talky and has too many names for bedtime.]
