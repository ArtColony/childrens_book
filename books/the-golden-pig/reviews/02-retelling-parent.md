# Retelling draft 1: The Golden Pig (parent)

*reviewer-parent (Sonnet), briefed with children of 6 and 8, 2026-11-14. Saved unchanged.*

**Lesson:** When people whisper cruel things about you because of where you came from, what you do matters more than what they say, and the grown-ups who believed the whispers should say sorry.

**1. Verdict**
I'd buy it. I wouldn't hide it, because the refrain and the father's turn hold up on the twentieth read. I'd only hide it if my 8-year-old kept asking "but was he the pig's son?" and the book had no answer.

**2. Line edits**
- **P6** "pressed the deer-hide cord of her pouch to the back of its head" is a stumble, and the pouch is never planted. Rewrite: "she touched a strip of deerskin to the back of its head." Plant the deerskin on p2 or p5. Add "and every stolen wife walked out" after "At dawn the rock opened."
- **P9** "the animals kept away from him" can't be read as either protection or shunning. Rewrite: "no wild beast dared come near him."
- **P12** The mother vanishes after p11. Add "His mother ran to him."
- **P16** "By then the boy had come to the capital" doesn't say why. Rewrite: "had gone to the capital to study." Cut "Minister Na" to "the minister".
- **P19-20** The clue is too thin and "has a voice" is obscure. p19: "The box had travelled many days. Eggs do not stay eggs." p20: "'You put in an egg. By now it has hatched: a chick.'"
- **P23** "Chiwon stepped aside" doesn't fit soup. Rewrite: "Each time a charm grew warm, and Chiwon was safe." Or better, "and Chiwon did not drink."
- **P23b** "gave in" becomes "gave up testing him". The ministers' whisper needs a consequence: "'The golden pig's child, they say. Send him away.'"
- **P25** "knew he had been wrong" has no cause. Rewrite: "When he found the poems in the sand, the Emperor knew he had been wrong."
- **P27** The strange fingernails (p7) are never paid off. Rewrite: "He took his son's hands, strange fingernails and all."

**3. Concerns, in priority order**
1. **Unanswered question.** Was he the pig's child? The book dodges it. Say plainly, in the text or back matter, that it doesn't matter, or my kids will ask me mid-page.
2. **Abandonment (p8).** The turn from p7 to p8 is well placed, and p9 rescues him quickly enough that bedtime survives. But the father's apology on p12 comes fast, and the boy forgives it in one beat. Give that moment one more breath, such as the boy looking at his father before he "came."
3. **Unearned reversal at p23b-25.** Exile and rescue both happen offstage. A 6-year-old will ask why the Emperor believed a whisper, then why he stopped.
4. **Moral muddle at the end.** The whispers stop because he becomes an immortal, not because people learn to judge by deeds. Only the father learns that. A child could take away "be extraordinary to be accepted." The p27 line is the real thesis, so keep it and don't bury it.
5. **Unyeong** waits, then walks up the mountain. Give her one act of her own, such as keeping the garden or answering the whispers.
6. **Too many names** (Munchang, Woryeongdae, Gaya, Tang, Silla). Add a pronunciation key.

**4. What works**
- The refrain and its turn on p28 are a joy to perform. Kids will chant it.
- "You threw me away. I will not come." lets a child be angry, and the father's later line, "I should have watched what you did," gives the adults something to think about.
- The violence is handled well. The wife kills the pig by wit and the pig's death is a single sentence. The poison is a bowl of soup, and the charm warms before any harm lands. The plants and payoffs (the soy cotton, the sand writing, the thread) are satisfying.
