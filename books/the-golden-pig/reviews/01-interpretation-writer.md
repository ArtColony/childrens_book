# Stage 1 review: The Golden Pig (writer)

*reviewer-writer (Opus) on `01-interpretation.md`, first draft, 2026-11-14.
The reviewer has no file-writing tool, so its reply is saved here unchanged.*

**Concept.** It's fresh in English. No picture-book *Choe Goun jeon* seems to exist (research.md found no English translation of the novel at all), and bilingual Korean folktales have shelf precedent (Suzanne Crowder Han's 1990s Holt books). The closer risk is the house's last book. *The Ugly Duckling* said "You are still growing into who you are." This book differs only if it never reveals what he "really" is. The kept mystery is that difference, so protect it.

**The heart: laid on top for now.** The plan names no choice the boy makes. The source's own drive is "genius will out." Yet the source gives him four acts:
- At three he refuses to come home because "his parents threw him away" (beat 14).
- He names the pavilion.
- He takes the name Chiwon.
- He says the box holds a chick, not the egg that went in.

That last one is the book. It's a sealed box (stone, in EN-WP), and everyone asks what is inside. The answer is what grew there, not what was put in. It rhymes with the rock that "opens at night." The plan files it as "one detour." It should be the climax.

There are two leaks:
- Sunflowers that "turn to follow him… bloom where he is protected" mark him as special from birth, which the plan's own "What it is not" rules out. Let them answer people's kindness, not his presence.
- The heavenly maidens and scholars risk swapping pig-birth for heaven-birth. Keep "letters in the sand" his own work, and draw it.

**Structure and refrain.** "Night for the pig (pages 1–8)" uses 8 of 28 pages. That leaves about 20 pages for about 15 episodes, which becomes a list. The China stretch sags worst. Talismans and soy-soaked cotton save him, so it tests magic, not him. Cutting the mirror trick also removes one of his few acts of wit.
- **Cut:** the Tang scholars, the exam, the blue lion, and one of the two women on the road.
- **Keep exile only if slander causes it.** Slander is the whisper again.
- **Tell the pig in five pages.**

The refrain is the right spine: it carries the pig past its death. Two problems:
- At the Tang court the fixed wording needs a reason, such as the scholars carrying the rumour home.
- "The boy answers it in a line of his own" is blank, and it is the book's most important line.

For reviewer-korean to check: 이래 is hearsay ("they say"), and the English drops it. "The golden pig's child, they say." keeps it.

**The father's line: wrong place, wrong verb.** process.md says the lesson goes "on the last story page" and is "shown before it is said." At the pavilion the boy is three and has done nothing yet. "I should have asked who you are" comes unearned and then leaves twelve pages with the heart already resolved. "Who you are" also contradicts the heart's "who you become." The father is still the right speaker, because he was the one who was wrong. But his repentance must keep coming before any success. Otherwise the book says worth equals achievement.

**The ending.** The rhyme is there but unused. The pig dragged wives into a mountain at night. Choe walks into one with his wife, freely, at golden evening. Paint it as a mirror of page 1. One warning: to adults, a hat and shoes left by a stream reads as drowning. Hang the hat on a pine and send a path into cloud.

**Also sinking:** "if he may marry Na's daughter" sits badly in a book that opens with stolen wives. Let Unyeong choose him. She can be the first person, after his mother, to judge him by his work in the garden.

## Three changes

1. **Make the box the climax** (about page 20, the widest spread). Ask the Emperor's riddle in the town's words: "What is inside?" The boy writes: *"You put in an egg. What is inside now has a voice: a chick."* Paint the stone box like the rock crevice.

2. **Turn the refrain on its hearsay.** On the last page: *"Once they said: the golden pig's child. Now they say: he became an immortal."* In Korean: *금돼지의 자식이래… 이제는 신선이 되었대.* What people say followed what he did. Nothing is settled, but the whisper changed. That is surprising and inevitable. Earlier, the boy answers the whisper with *"My name is Chiwon."* (致遠, "reaching far." reviewer-korean should confirm the gloss.)

3. **Move the father's lesson to the end.** At the pavilion he gives only an apology: *"I was afraid of a story. Come home."* On the next-to-last page, old, watching his son go into the mountain, he says: *"When you were born, I listened to what people said. I should have watched what you did."* That is earned by the whole book, and it says "did," not "are."

**Score: 5/10 as planned.** It could reach about 7.5 if the box becomes the climax, the boy gets his line and the father's line moves to the end.
