# Retelling draft 1: The Golden Pig (writer)

*reviewer-writer (Opus), 2026-11-14. Saved unchanged.*

**Stage 2 review: *The Legend of the Golden Pig*, retelling draft 1**

Three of the lines judged here are mine from stage 1: the chick line (p.20), the father's line (p.27) and the turned refrain (p.28). I hold them to the same standard, and two of them are flawed.

**Heart.** It now comes from the boy's acts through p.20. "You threw me away. I will not come." (p.11) is the best line in the book. After p.20 he goes passive. He is given gifts (p.22), and "a charm grew warm, and Chiwon stepped aside" (p.23). Then he is sent away, called back, and "A blue lion carried him home." The highest danger falls on the pages where he does least.

**Box.** On the page it is nearly a true climax, but p.19 gives it away: "But things change in the dark." That is the book's idea in the narrator's mouth, one page before the chick shows it. In the structure the box falls at p.20 of 28, and the exile and poison come after it, so it reads as a midpoint. The fix is to give him acts after it (change 3).

**Refrain.** The verbs build well: "whispered," "muttered," "sniffed," "told." Three of its four uses have consequences: abandonment, refusal, exile. The p.16 use has none, so it goes stale there. P.17 answers it by telling: "did not care what anyone said."

**P.28 turn.** It lands as a rhyme, but in my own wording "he became an immortal" follows from his vanishing, not from anything he did. That proves rumours change, not that deeds decide. "An immortal" is also abstract for a six-year-old. And "were never seen again" is a cliché that the hat already shows.

**Father's line (mine).** It has a logic slip: "When you were born… I should have watched what you did." A newborn has done nothing to watch. The line is earned only in part. The father has been off the page since p.12, and the text never says where this scene happens. P.8's "he would not listen" is a good seed; keep it.

**Slack pages and lines that trip:**
- p.9: "the animals of the shore kept away from him." This makes him monstrous for no reason. Cut it.
- p.15: "No one could answer" repeats "no one can see inside a stone." Cut it.
- p.16: "By then the boy had come to the capital." A magistrate's son is suddenly a servant, with no reason given. Make it a choice: "chose to work off the debt."
- p.23b: "So the Emperor gave in" is flat.
- p.26 crams a wedding, a rejection and a turn toward the mountains onto one page. "looked toward the mountains" is passive.
- p.21: "I will wait." / "I will come back." is serviceable but generic.
- p.16 is at the 38-word cap.

**Page turns.** 4→5, 14→18 and 19→20 work. "Cheep." is a perfect page-end. 23→23b sags, and 26→27 has no hinge.

**Also:** the mother kills the pig, weeps and begs, then vanishes. The father gets the whole reconciliation.

**Three changes**

1. **P.19: make the boy listen instead of the narrator explaining.** The father listened to what people said; the boy listens to the thing itself. This does not settle who his father was.
   > "He held the cold stone box to his ear and listened for a long time. Then he dipped his brush and wrote."

   That is 21 words, down from 31. P.20's "has a voice" now pays it off.

2. **P.27: fix the logic slip, and seed the line on p.17.**
   > p.27: "His father was very old now. He took his son's hands. 'I listened to what people said about you. I should have watched what you did.'"

   That is 26 words, down from 28. On p.17, replace the last clause:
   > "The minister's daughter, Unyeong, watched what he did, not what they said."

   P.17 drops to 26 words. The father now learns what Unyeong knew all along.

3. **Give him acts after the box (pp.25 and 28).**
   > p.25: "At last the Emperor knew he had been wrong, and begged him to stay. Chiwon bowed. 'No. I have a promise to keep.' A blue lion carried him home across the sea."

   That is 32 words, and it echoes his refusal on p.11. On p.28, tie the new saying to his poems in the sand, and cut "never seen again":
   > "One golden evening Chiwon and Unyeong walked up Mount Gaya into the clouds. Only his hat hung on a pine. Once they said: the golden pig's child. Now they read his poems and say: he lives forever."

   That is 37 words. What people say now follows from what he did, and the mystery of his father stays open.

**Score: 6.5/10** against award-quality picture books, up from 5. With these three changes it would reach about 7.5. Getting past that would need the China stretch to test him rather than his charms. You overruled me on that, so I note it and don't press it.
