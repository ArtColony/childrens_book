# Retelling draft 1: The Golden Pig (educator)

*reviewer-educator (Sonnet), briefed for a first- and second-grade class, 2026-11-14. Saved unchanged.*

**Review: *The Legend of the Golden Pig*, draft 1 (class of 6-8)**

**1. Developmental fit**
- Sentences run 8-15 words, which suits this age. At 919 words the text is well within what 6-8s can hold.
- These words need support: *magistrate* (say "town governor"), *vanished*, *pouch*, *shuddered*, *maidens*, *pavilion*, *terrace*, *sealed*, *minister*, *charms*, *barren*, *immortal*.
- I'd give the concepts before the first read.
  - Silla is "a kingdom in Korea more than a thousand years ago".
  - Tang is "the huge empire next door".
  - A seal is something you break, so bring a real wax seal to show.
  - An immortal is a mountain spirit who never dies.
- p.14: "Sealed stone box" is fine once the picture shows the seal.

**2. Text against picture**
- **p.6:** The "deer-hide cord of her pouch" is never planted. Show the pouch on p.2 or p.3 so p.5's "only deerskin" pays off. Also, who opens the rock at dawn?
- **p.8:** "His wife wept" isn't in the picture, which shows only the baby and the father.
- **p.12:** The Korean says 헛소문 ("a false rumour"). The English says "a story". That quietly settles the mystery the book promises to leave open. Change the Korean to 소문.
- **p.16:** The boy's age is never stated (3, then 10, then 12). Give it in the text.
- **p.18:** "Lose his head" is harsher than the Korean 목숨 ("life"). Use "life".
- **p.19:** "The cold stone box" works against a hatching egg. Cut "cold".
- **p.23:** "Chiwon stepped aside" doesn't work for soup. Use "pushed the bowl away".
- **p.26:** "The court did not want him" and "people whispered" can't be drawn on a wedding page.
- **Cultural checks:** Verify "Unyeong" against the novel, because it's also the heroine of a different novel, 운영전. I could not confirm this from the files. Draw hanja, not hangul, for the sand letters, since hangul came about 600 years later.

**3. Emotional literacy**
- Fear is named for the father (p.8) and the Emperor (p.14). Point out the parallel to the class.
- The boy's own feelings are never named. Hurt, loneliness and anger are only implied by "You threw me away" (p.11), and the exile on p.24 has none.
- Add one line on p.24, such as "He was hungry and alone, but he wrote". Writing in the sand is good coping and echoes p.10.
- The apology on p.12 comes early and owns the fear. The boy just "came", though, so pause for how he might feel.
- p.26 risks teaching children to withdraw ("looked toward the mountains") after rejection.
- p.28 "were never seen again" echoes p.1 "vanished". Expect "Did they die?" Prepare the answer: the legend says they became mountain spirits, together and by choice.

**4. Violence**
- It is well handled. The pig dies in one line and the wife is the agent.
- The abandonment on p.8 is the heaviest page. It is eased by the turn to p.9, where the child is safe.
- The poison is mild, since no one is hurt.
- On p.9, "animals kept away from him" can read as shunning. Say "let him be".

**5. Read-aloud**
- **Join-in:**
  - the refrain on pp.7, 11, 16 and 23b, whispered by the whole class;
  - the wind and the owl on pp.3-4;
  - "Cheep" on p.20.
- **Pauses:**
  - p.5: why does she ask "sweetly"?
  - p.8: how do we feel about the father?
  - p.11: why won't he come?
  - p.19: what could change inside a box? Let them guess before p.20.
- **Where I'd lose them:**
  - pp.1-3, which are abstract setup with no child in them;
  - pp.16-17, the mirror and the garden;
  - the trap parade on p.23;
  - the wedding on p.26;
  - the dense "Once they said... Now they say" on p.28.
- **Sittings:** Two. The first runs pp.0-15, with about 10 minutes of reading and pauses in one language. It stops at "No one could answer", which is a good cliffhanger. The second runs pp.16-29. Reading both languages doubles the time.
- Counts are right: three charms on p.22 and three traps on p.23.

**6. Extension**
Run a "mystery box". Seal a box, let the children shake it and guess, then write their answer with a brush, as Chiwon did. Follow with a "whisper turned around" circle where children say what people said about them and what they actually did.

**Recommendation:** Yes, for the library at ages 7-9 and for a teacher-led read-aloud at 6-8, once pp.6, 12 (Korean), 16, 19, 23 and 26 are fixed.
