# Stage 3: Storyboard

Every page is a thumbnail at full trim size (`03-storyboard/NN_*.ps`). Each
one uses:

- the frame from `lib/cb_goldpig.ps` (`cb-gp-panel`), with the painting in
  the panel;
- the **real text**, set from `text.ps` by `cb-gp-say`, so text fit and
  widows are real;
- the real people, pig and motif procedures at the right size and facing.

The contact sheet is made with `scripts/sheet.py`.

**What the thumbnails predict.** This book's motifs are flat minhwa colour
(`cb-wash`, `cb-mh-flat`), not big washes, so `cb-rough` (which greys only
`cb-bigwash`) would leave most of a page in colour anyway. The board is
therefore drawn **in colour, at low detail**, with real procedures where
they exist and plain placeholders where they don't. It predicts:

- composition, scale, facing and text fit, exactly;
- value structure and colour roughly.

It does not predict pattern and texture density, which the painting adds.

**Folios:** none. A fourth English line sits at about y 39, and a folio at
y 22 would crowd it. Many picture books go without page numbers, and the
back matter can't be numbered either.

**Shot runs:** no more than three of the same in a row. Pages 1–4 run W C M W
and pages 24–29 run M W W M C W.

**Page 20, the climax, against the fixed layout.** The reader's layout has
two text bands, and page 20 carries 3 Korean and 3 English lines. So page 20
is the one page where **the painting bleeds off all four edges** and the two
texts sit on hanji panels (`cb-panel`) laid over the painting. That is the
biggest turn in the book, and the text stays in its usual places.
