# Stage 2 retelling, draft 1: parent (reviewer-parent, Sonnet, text only)

**LESSON:** "When others are cruel about how you look and what you can do, it is not your fault, and you can survive it and find where you belong, because you were never ugly, only different and still growing."

**Would buy it,** after two fixes:

1. **Worth is tied to the outcome (pp. 16–17).** "Still growing" is "the
   'you'll be beautiful later' trap": a child hears "wait and you'll get
   pretty". The fix is "he was a swan all along". Cut "I never dreamed of
   such happiness, when I was the ugly duckling", which puts the label
   back on him and makes beauty the prize. Belonging is the reward. p16's
   "not a clumsy grey bird any more" agrees with the bullies.
2. **"Ugly" as a label:** use it only in the bullies' mouths.

**Also:**

- No adult ever fixes the bullying, and running away can read as "run and
  hide". Let the mother's "look how well he swims" come back once.
- The lonely middle runs long.
- The cottage and the flyover could be merged.

**What works:**

- p11 ("loved them more than he had ever loved anything") "is the heart of
  the book".
- The wordless p13 and the last page: "restraint that trusts the child".
- p15, the bowing swans: "the kindest page in the book".
