# Stage 1b style pick: art director (reviewer-artist, Sonnet)

**Verdict: B (painted), with C's flat-shape discipline.** The brief is
"beautiful", and the medium is strongest at sky, light and water. B is the
only study where those three carry the picture. A is safe but flat. C is
the most graphic, but it is mechanical across 16 pages and can't do mist
or ice, which winter needs.

**Fix or push in B before painting:**

1. **Trees:** use willow and reed-bed silhouettes with feathered tops, thin
   trunks and mist in front. Drop the domes, which read as rocks.
2. **The duckling** is the hero and the weakest element. Give him a round
   fluffy body, a tuft, a catch-light eye, a warm beak accent and a
   reflection. Draw him once, in a library procedure, as Moss is.
3. **Horizon:** soften it with mist and reflected colour, and keep it low.
4. **Foreground:** add a dark frame of overlapping reeds and cattails.
5. **Values:** the swans brightest, the duckling a mid-dark shape on light
   water. Three values and one focal point per page.
6. **Texture:** ripples of varied length that widen toward us, and a quiet
   sky band for the type.

**Seasons, one look:** keep the horizon, grain, water, reed frame, duckling
and text band constant. Vary the palette, the light and one signature
element:

- **Summer:** greens and gold.
- **Autumn:** violet, rose and amber, the emotional high point.
- **Winter:** blue-grey and lilac, ice, almost no warmth.
- **Spring:** pale greens and blossom, brighter. The white swan echoes the
  autumn flock.
