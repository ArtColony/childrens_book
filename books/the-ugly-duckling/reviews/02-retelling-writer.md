# Stage 2 retelling, draft 1: editor (reviewer-writer, Opus, briefed for a retelling and an explicit message)

**Score: 6/10.** "Faithful and well judged, and the structure is ready for the art. The prose is serviceable rather than musical, about a third of the words repeat the pictures, and the message line needs to be an answer, not a speech."

- **Comparables:** Jerry Pinkney's *The Ugly Duckling* (Caldecott Honor, 1999). For leaving things to the art, his wordless *The Lion & the Mouse*.
- **The cuts are right:** the hunters, the farmer, the mother's curse, and above all the swan's-egg line.
- **The middle sags:** pp. 6–9 are three rejections in a row. **The old woman is a person**, and the note cut the farmer for exactly that reason. Cut her and make p7 wordless.
- **Best sentence:** "he loved them, more than he had ever loved anything" (p11). The swimming thread pays off.
- **About 465 words is too long** for an art-led book for ages 3–6; aim for 280–320. Cut what the paintings show (pp. 1, 10, 14, 16). p14 also gives away the reveal, so keep him grey in the art.
- **Welcome before reflection is the strongest decision in the book.** The bow is the mechanism: they bow, he bows back, and so he sees himself.
- **p17:**
  - The swans say the line unprompted. Let him speak first, so theirs is an answer.
  - Drop "only", which makes difference sound like a smaller fault.
  - Don't end the book on the words "the ugly duckling".
