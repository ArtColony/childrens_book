# The Ugly Duckling: reflection

## 1. What the book says

**The line it says out loud:** *"You were never ugly. You were different.
And you were still growing."* The swans say it, as an answer to his own
whisper, and the last words are an invitation: *"Come, swim with us."*

**What the cold readers heard:**

| Reader | Stage | Lesson, in their words |
|---|---|---|
| Child | retelling, text only | "Even when you look different, you can still be lovely, and you might be growing into something really wonderful." |
| Child | finished book, images only | "Even when you are different and other people are mean, you can keep going and grow up to be wonderful." |
| Adult | finished book, images only | "Being different isn't the same as being bad, and you'll find where you belong while you're still growing." |

Every reader heard "different, and growing", none heard "born better". The
trap Andersen's last line sets (worth comes from a swan's egg) was cut at
stage 1, and it stayed cut.

**What it still risks:** a child can hear "ugly now, pretty later". The
first child reader said it about draft 1 ("and then you turn out to be a
swan!"). The fix was to put the welcome *before* the reveal: the swans bow
to a grey bird on p15. On the finished book the child's answer moved to
"keep going", which is closer to the tale's real heart: he survives the
winter himself, with the gift his mother named.

## 2. Separating the art from the story

The experiment: take a story that already works, and spend the stages on
the look.

- **What worked:** the style studies (three looks of one scene) chose the
  book's voice before any page was painted, and the season palettes
  (`cb-season`) gave the book an arc you can see on the contact sheet:
  summer gold, autumn violet, winter navy, spring blue. The growth row
  (`cb-dkgrow`) made one bird visibly grow. The artist's three strongest
  pages are the nearly wordless ones (p7, p13, p14): when the story is
  known, the pictures can carry it.
- **What didn't:** flat painted shapes are clean but simple. The artist
  scored 7/10. The
  pages rely on one water-and-horizon composition, and the shimmering
  horizon band repeats on six pages. A painted style with no line needs
  more variety of camera and depth than the house style, not less.

## 3. Process

- **Advisor at every gate** (Puff had it only at the ends).
- **Codex was unavailable at the end.** A Claude code reviewer stood in. It
  found three real bugs we would not have seen on the page: reeds drawn as
  hooked double strokes, marks in the text band, and the mist costing 1 MB
  per page (21 → 13 MB). The substitution is logged in the process doc, so
  a Codex pass can be run later.
- **Type moves:** the rule from Puff (one reading path, one move per page)
  held, but two moves fought their pictures: p16's mirrored line and p11's
  rising line. Both were cut back. A type move has to stay out of the
  page's main image.

## 4. A second painting pass would

1. Vary the horizon: lower and higher water lines, and a close shot in the
   reeds.
2. Make p5 a real run under the hedge, with the bird larger.
3. Make p9's door a door: a frame and spilled light.
4. Give p17 one hero swan, slightly larger, and no cropping.
5. Lose the ear tufts, which read as a cat's ears.
6. Bring the PDF under 10 MB (`cb-psky` is the next largest cost).
